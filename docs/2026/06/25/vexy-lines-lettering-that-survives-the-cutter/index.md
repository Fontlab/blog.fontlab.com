Airbrushed lettering dies at the vinyl cutter: no edge to follow, no colour to separate. The [Vexy Lines case study: lettering](https://www.vexy.art/lines/case-lettering/) rebuilds the glow as flat vector structure — halftone stars sized by tone, masked outlines, hand-tuned Béziers. The result cuts cleanly at four feet, separates to spot colours, and stays editable the whole way.

We turned a soft, low-res airbrush mark into ultrasharp vectors using seven Vexy Lines fills. Best part? Not one of them uses an actual gradient or pixel texture!

A halftone fill scatters stars whose size tracks the tone of the original, so the glow becomes dots you can cut. Hand-drawn shells get their outer edge auto-traced, then tightened by hand with the simple Vexy Lines curve editor. We added a teal core by masking and shrinking the outline, plus a darker halftone fill inside the same mask for texture.

Every soft transition is now a hard-edged path or a field of countable marks. That is the difference between a picture of your wordmark, and a wordmark that the machine can actually reproduce.
