## Writing

Each blog post needs to be its own story. Follow the style guide below — but make each one appropriate for the target audience! 

<STYLE_GUIDE>
WRITING RULES: 

Hook fast. No throat-clearing.

Cut jargon. Cut passive voice. Cut the corporate fog and the hype words that mean nothing. If a reader has to read a sentence twice, rewrite it once.

Show, don’t gesture. A line of code beats a paragraph about code. A real example beats a category. When you describe what a tool does, describe what it does to a file, a glyph, a Tuesday afternoon.

Humor is welcome. Hammering it is not. Clarity wins every fight with cleverness.

Weave the technical into the human. The kerning table is a promise to the reader’s eye. The vector path is a hand the designer can hold. Specs sell when they carry a stake.

Forget fiction templates. Steal instead from three working writers: King, who cuts adverbs like weeds. Zinsser, who builds non-fiction the way a carpenter builds stairs — one clear step at a time. Ogilvy, who never forgot that the next word has to earn the click. And add Mary Oliver, who knew that a true sentence has weather in it.

Every word performs. Or every word goes.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​
</STYLE_GUIDE>

ADDENDUM TO STYLE GUIDE: 

Make FRIENDLIER tone and be MORE CONVERSATIONAL. Relax the rules made by STYLE_GUIDE. Approach each and every blog post with an individual twist, slightly different writing and language convention. Make it suitable per audience, and make it work with the kinds of media we choose.
