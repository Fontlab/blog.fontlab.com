In 1977, an East German print shop turned a technical limitation into an enduring style: typographer Albert Kapr used the linear halftone — parallel strokes that thicken in shadow and thin in light — because the cheap ink and fast press would smudge anything else. Vexy Lines brings that engraving logic to the screen as vectors: feed it a grayscale photo and one Linear fill draws the entire poster, then recalculates the moment you change a parameter. The same source image yields three directions — a faithful redraw, a color pass, or a brutalist stipple — and every one exports as SVG that scales cleanly from gig poster to billboard.
