If you read about FontLab only in English, you get one picture.
If you read about it in Polish, Spanish, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, and German, you get
six.

<!-- more -->

<!-- image TBD -->

The German trade press tends to focus on production rigour.
Power Brush and the Lever get praise as tools that finally let calligraphic designers
experiment fluidly without losing the precision the format demands.
There is an emphasis on hinting, on Microsoft ClearType preview, on the boring
engineering parts that keep a font from breaking in Word.

Spanish-language reviews lean toward the variable-fonts story.
A Runebook primer puts it bluntly: the bottleneck is no longer technology, it is whether
your typeface is ready.
Pixartprinting’s Italian and Spanish blog posts agree: variable fonts are not a feature
you tick on the brief, they are a discipline.

Japanese coverage — `ics.media`, `note.com`, the JustFont team in Taiwan — is closer to
typography theory.
The width axis comparison from JustFont, showing brute-force squeezing
next to a designed condensed, says more about variable fonts than most English articles
ever will. There is a strong Hanzi presence too: ATypI 2020 talks on digital revivals of
Chinese Buddhist manuscripts and the integration of Chinese cultural motifs into modern
Hanzi design.

Korean writing leans on design-system framing.
Roboto Flex appears constantly.
So do discussions of slant, optical clarity, and fine-grained parameters as
design-system tokens.
The argument is that a variable font is not a typography feature; it is a tool for
hierarchy.

Polish coverage from `typoteka.pl`, `grafmag.pl`, and `designpractice.pl` is more
historical. Variable fonts are placed in the long arc from screen typography to the
modern web. Tools like FontLab and TransType show up as preservation infrastructure as
much as production tools.

The composite picture is more useful than any single regional view.
FontLab is a calligraphy tool, a variable-font factory, a Cyrillic-and-CJK-capable
production environment, a hinting engine, and a preservation utility.
Different languages emphasise different parts.
The software has to do all of it.
