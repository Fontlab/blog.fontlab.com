In January 2000, a new company — Fontlab Ltd. — bought all the assets of Pyrus North America Ltd. and continued development of the FontLab product line.

<!-- more -->

Pyrus North America had operated since 1992 as the international face of SoftUnion's font software. By 1995, SoftUnion had divested its software business to concentrate on hardware, selling all rights to FontLab to Pyrus along with the programming team. Yuri Yarmola, who had led the original SoftUnion development group, stayed on to continue development under Pyrus.

The decade between 1992 and 2000 saw steady expansion: FontLab 3.0 for Windows (1998, a complete rewrite with custom TrueType hinting and native cubic B-spline editing), the first Mac product in late 1998 (a port of FontLab Composer for CJK font editing), and TransType in early 1999 (a Mac application for converting fonts between Windows and Mac formats).

In January 2000, Fontlab Ltd. was incorporated and took ownership of the full product line. Ted Harrison became President; Yuri Yarmola continued as lead developer. The St. Petersburg team remained in place. The company name made explicit what had already been the brand — the font software was already called FontLab.

The first releases under the Fontlab Ltd. name were TransType 2 and BitFonter, both in 2001. BitFonter addressed bitmap font editing, a niche that had been underserved; TransType 2 extended the font conversion capability introduced in the original 1999 Mac release.
